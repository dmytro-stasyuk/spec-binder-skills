# Extracting a composite step

You take imperative steps, which walk through how the system is driven or inspected, and put them under a new
declarative step that states the business fact they set up, carry out or check: a SpecBinder **composite step**,
with the steps as its `*` sub-steps. The scenario then reads as business facts, while the test still does what it
did.

This is a pure refactoring. The generated test still calls the same step methods, in the same order, with the same
values, and no step code changes. So never reword, merge, drop or reorder a step: the only change to a step you put
under a parent is that the values the parent now states reach it through references.
[composite-steps.md](references/composite-steps.md) says how composite steps work, which steps go under one parent,
and how to write the parent and its sub-steps. Read it first.

Keywords in this skill stand for whatever the spec uses: `Ability:` for any top-level keyword (`Feature`,
`Ability`, `Business Need`, `Narrative`), `Example:` for `Scenario` too and for an outline, and each keyword in the
spec's own language when a `# language:` header sets one. `Given`, `When` and `Then` name a step's role: a step
written with `And`, `But` or `*` takes the role of the step before it.

## Options

- `--only-lines=<lines>`: the lines of the spec file to work on, such as `--only-lines=7` or `--only-lines=12-30`,
  counting from 1; several lines or ranges are separated by commas. Typically the lines selected in the editor, or
  the caret's line with nothing selected. A request can instead name Examples, Rules or a `Background:` by title.
  Without either, the whole of each spec file given. Section 2 says what the lines pick.
- `--step="<wording>"`: the new step's wording, such as `--step="the customer checks out"`, or the same said in the
  request ("make these a composite step: the customer checks out"). It is for the steps given (section 2): given
  blocks, ask which steps it is for. Without it, you word the step yourself.
- `--move-all-parameters-up`: the new step states every quoted value of the steps it replaces, labels included, and
  takes over every data table and doc string they have. By default it states only the values the Example depends
  on.
- `--only-selection`: change only what the lines or titles pick. By default the same steps elsewhere in the spec go
  under the same new step too, as section 5 says.
- `--verify`: build and run the spec's tests before and after, and compare each Example's result, as
  [section 7](#7-verify-with---verify) says. Off by default.
- `--as-review-finding`: publish the change to the spec's AI Review tab in IntelliJ IDEA as findings, for the
  author to apply or dismiss, instead of writing it to the file, as
  [section 8](#8-as-review-findings-with---as-review-finding) says. `--verify` doesn't apply then: nothing changes
  until the author applies a finding. Say so in the report when the request has it.

## 1. Check the spec can take composite steps

Composite steps are generated by SpecBinder's Java annotation processor. When the project isn't a Java project,
stop, and say why.

## 2. Find what to change

Work on one spec file at a time. Given several files or a folder, take every spec file among them, the files and
folders as the request gives them and beneath a folder its own spec files first, alphabetically, then each of its
folders, the same way down. That is the order the IDE's Project view lists them in. Read each spec file in full.

What is given picks either **steps** or **blocks**.

**Steps.** When every line given is on steps (a step's lines include its data table or doc string), they pick
those steps. They must follow one another in one Example or `Background:`; when they don't, ask which to take.
The steps picked become **one** composite step:
- When they look like more than one fact, or cross from one role to another (`Given` to `When`, `When` to `Then`),
  make the one composite step anyway, since the author picked them. Work out too how you would group them, as
  composite-steps.md says, to offer in the report, or as a finding of its own with `--as-review-finding`.
- When some of them are a composite step or its sub-steps, leave those as they are: the steps picked before them
  become one composite step and those after them another. Say so in the report.
- When only some of them are imperative, make the one composite step anyway: the author picked them. When none of
  them is, say they already state business facts and ask whether to put them under a new step anyway.

**Blocks.** Anything else picks blocks, and you find and group their imperative steps yourself, as composite-steps.md
says, one composite step per fact. A line given takes in:
- the Example it is on, when it is on its title or the tags and comments just above it, or when the lines take in
  every line of it;
- the `Background:` it is on, when it is on its `Background:` line;
- the whole Rule, its `Background:` and every Example in it, when it is on one of the Rule's own lines before its
  first Example: its title or description;
- the whole spec, when it is on one of the spec's own lines before its first Rule or Example, such as its `Ability:`
  line or story.

In an Example or `Background:` that the lines take in only partly, such as the first of several Examples a selection
runs across, group only the steps they pick. A title in the request picks the Example, Rule or `Background:` it
names; a file or folder given without lines, every spec in it.

If the lines run past the end of the file, or pick nothing (the blank lines between Examples, say), ask: the file may
have changed since they were taken. If a title matches nothing, or matches several, ask.

The steps that become sub-steps are the imperative ones, as composite-steps.md says. When the project's review
guidelines (`.specbinder/review-guidelines.md`, and the `.md` files in `.specbinder/review-guidelines/`) have a check
that flags imperative steps, such as the starter guidelines' `declarative-steps` and `implementation-detail`, read it
and follow it too.

## 3. Write each composite step

Write the parent step and its sub-steps as composite-steps.md says, with all parameters when the request has
`--move-all-parameters-up`. Word the places with the same steps together, as it says, before writing any of them.

With `--step`, or a wording in the request, use that wording. Every quoted value in it must reach a sub-step: when
one matches no value of the steps it replaces, ask the author, suggesting it without quotes or with the value the
steps have. Otherwise word it yourself. Across the specs of one run, a fact gets the same wording every time.

## 4. Check what the parent hides

Before going on, read each Example you change as its reader will, with the sub-steps folded away, as the IDE can
show them: the parent steps and the steps left. It must still say everything its title and its Rule are about. When a
value that matters is now hidden in a sub-step, state it on the parent.

## 5. The same steps elsewhere in the spec

Unless the request has `--only-selection`, look through the rest of the spec, every Example and `Background:`, for
the same steps: the same step methods, in the same order, next to each other and in one role, which aren't a
composite step or its sub-steps already. Their values may differ. Put each place under the same parent step, worded
the same, with that place's own values, as composite-steps.md says. The places found here don't change the parent's
wording or values: the places given decide them. Leave a place as it is, and name it in the report with the reason,
when:
- read with its sub-steps folded away, as section 4 says, its Example would no longer say everything its title and
  its Rule are about, or would hide a value it depends on: the password in an Example about a wrong password, or
  `pay with the saved card` in "Paying with the saved card skips the card form";
- the parent's wording outside quotes isn't true of it, such as `Alice is signed in` where Bob signs in.

Name the near-matches too, without changing them: places with the same steps but for a step more in between, or one
missing, such as `apply the discount code "<code>"` between `open the cart` and `confirm the delivery address`.
Offer to put each under a parent that takes in the difference: the same wording, when it is still true of the place
and the extra step holds nothing its Example depends on; otherwise a wording of its own that states it, such as
`When the customer checks out with the discount code "<code>"`.

Look through the other spec files in the spec's own folder too, but not those in the folders below it or anywhere
else. A place in a spec file given in this run is changed as part of it. Name the places in other spec files in the
report, without changing them.

## 6. Write the change

First check every sub-step against the step it was, as composite-steps.md's "Checking the sub-steps" says, and
correct the parent or its sub-steps until each one reads as it did.

With `--verify`, run the tests first, as [section 7](#7-verify-with---verify) says, before anything changes. With
`--as-review-finding`, publish the change as [section 8](#8-as-review-findings-with---as-review-finding) says
instead of writing it.

Make the change to each spec file as
[changing-a-spec-file.md](../review-spec/references/changing-a-spec-file.md) says: in the IDE's editor when you can,
every place in one call, each edit replacing the lines of the steps a parent replaces, from the first step's line to
the last line of the last step, its data table or doc string included, with the parent step and its sub-steps.

## 7. Verify, with `--verify`

[java-wiring.md](../automate-spec/references/java-wiring.md) says how a spec's test class is found and how to build
and run it; read it first. If the spec has no test class yet, it isn't wired: say so and skip the run.

1. **Before writing the file**, rebuild and run the spec's test class, and note each Example's result.
2. **After writing it**, rebuild and run the test class again.
3. **Each Example's result** should be the one it had before. Name any that isn't, with its failure. When the build
   fails on the new steps, composite steps are most likely not on for the spec yet: say so, as section 9 does.

## 8. As review findings, with `--as-review-finding`

Publish the change as findings, for the author to apply or dismiss in the IDE. The spec file stays as it is. This
needs the SpecBinder plugin's tools on the IDE's MCP server (`specbinder_get_review_scope`,
`specbinder_get_spec_review` and `specbinder_publish_spec_review`), used as the `review-spec` skill uses them.
Without them, write the change to the file instead, and say so.

1. **Check the reviews.** Call `specbinder_get_review_scope` and `specbinder_get_spec_review` for each spec.
   Publishing replaces a spec's current review. If `suggestion` is among the kinds to skip for a spec, or it still
   has open findings, ask the author once for the whole run, saying which specs and what. They can tick `suggestion`
   in the IDE, or let these findings replace the open ones, or both, and you publish; or you write the change to the
   files instead.
2. **Read each spec** through the IDE's file tool, not from disk: the findings name lines as the editor has them.
3. **Cut the change into findings**, each a decision the author takes or leaves:
   - **Steps picked:** one finding, whose fix makes the one composite step. When they look like more than one fact,
     or cross a role, a second finding with the same `line` and `quote`, whose fix makes one composite step per
     fact instead, in the same places. Say in each message that the other is its alternative. Applying either one
     changes the lines the other quotes, which closes it.
   - **One Example or `Background:` taken in:** one finding per new parent step, its fix putting the steps under it
     in that block and in every other place section 5 puts under it.
   - **More than one Example or `Background:` taken in**, by lines across several, a Rule, the spec's first lines,
     or a file or folder: one finding per Example or `Background:` that gets a composite step, its fix making all of
     that block's composite steps. A place outside them that section 5 puts under the same parent step gets a finding
     of its own in the same way.
   - **A near-match** that section 5 names: a finding of its own, its fix putting that place under the parent you
     offer for it. Say in its message how its steps differ from the others'.
4. **Publish** each spec's findings together, in one call, since each publish replaces the spec's review, built as
   `review-spec` builds a finding whose fix rewrites whole lines: see step 5 of its
   [SKILL.md](../review-spec/SKILL.md), and write each message as its "Writing for the author" says. Give each:
   - `line` and `quote`: the first line of the first step its fix replaces, and that step's text;
   - `kind` `suggestion`, `category` `extract-composite-step`, and no `check`, since no guideline raised it;
   - a fix of whole-lines edits, one per parent step, each replacing the steps that parent replaces with the parent
     and its sub-steps, as section 6 says. The edits must not overlap;
   - a message saying which steps walk through how the system is used and the fact they set up, carry out or check,
     and then, in a paragraph of its own, what the fix does: that the steps go under a new step stating that fact,
     unchanged but for the values it passes them, the values it states, and the other Examples and Backgrounds it
     changes too, named as section 9 names them.
5. **Correct and publish again** if the tool turns a finding down.

## 9. Report

Give:
- each new composite step's wording, where it went, by title: each Example, and each `Background:` as the spec's or
  its Rule's (`the Background of the Rule "Discount codes"`), and the values it states;
- for steps picked that look like more than one fact or cross a role, the composite steps you would make instead,
  with their wordings, and an offer to redo it that way; with `--as-review-finding`, the alternative finding's
  number;
- the places in the spec changed too, and those left as they were, with why; the places in other spec files with the
  same steps, which weren't changed;
- the near-matches, with what differs, and the parent you offer for each; with `--as-review-finding`, their
  findings' numbers;
- the steps left as they were because they already state a fact, and any composite step left as it was;
- the Examples and Backgrounds of each spec given that still have imperative steps outside what was given, by title,
  and an offer to do them too. Don't change them;
- that if composite steps aren't on for this spec yet, the author turns them on:
  `@Gherkin2JUnitOptions(enableCompositeSteps = true)` on its marker class. Until then, the build fails on the new
  steps. Turning them on also puts every step already written with `*`, in the specs that marker covers, under the
  step above it, which then only runs those `*` steps. Say this without checking whether they are on;
- that every sub-step, its references filled in, reads as the step it was;
- with `--verify`, the Examples' results, or what differed, or the build's error; without it, that nothing was
  compiled or run;
- with `--as-review-finding`, the findings' numbers, and that the author applies them in the AI Review tab.
